graphics for Figures 6-8). The full captions are in the manuscript; below, each figure is

figure number (and panel letter) used in the manuscript.

Figures 1-5 (showcases; output of the plot function for evSyn in restriktor)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
General layout of each panel: a line plot with the studies (1 to S) on the x-axis and the GORICA
weights (0 to 1) on the y-axis. For both hypotheses, H_123 (orange) and its complement (green),
the cumulative (overall) GORICA weight after adding each study is shown as a line with dots, and
the study-specific GORICA weight as triangles. A dashed horizontal line at 0.5 denotes equal support.
Within each figure, the left panels (a, c, e) show the equal-evidence approach and the right
panels (b, d, f) the added-evidence approach.

Figure 1 - H_123 (true) versus its complement, S studies with the same dataset (n_s = 85).
- Fig1a_evS_equal_123_compl_S4.pdf: equal-evidence, S = 4. Each study gives H_123 a weight of
  0.73; the cumulative weight for H_123 rises slowly to 0.82 after four studies.
- Fig1b_evS_added_123_compl_S4.pdf: added-evidence, S = 4. Same study-specific weights; the
  cumulative weight for H_123 rises quickly to 0.98.
- Fig1c_evS_equal_123_compl_S20.pdf: equal-evidence, S = 20. The cumulative weight for H_123
  rises gradually to 0.99.
- Fig1d_evS_added_123_compl_S20.pdf: added-evidence, S = 20. The cumulative weight for H_123
  reaches (practically) 1 within a few studies.

Figure 2 - H_123 (false) versus its (true) complement, S studies with the same dataset.
- Fig2a_evS_equal_123_compl_S4_Opp.pdf: equal-evidence, S = 4, n_s = 85. Each study slightly
  favours the false H_123 (0.54 vs 0.46), but the cumulative weight shifts towards the true
  complement, reaching 0.87.
- Fig2b_evS_added_123_compl_S4_Opp.pdf: added-evidence, S = 4, n_s = 85. The cumulative weight
  for the false H_123 increases to 0.65.
- Fig2c_evS_equal_123_compl_S20_Opp.pdf: equal-evidence, S = 20, n_s = 85. The cumulative
  weight for the true complement increases to 1.00.
- Fig2d_evS_added_123_compl_S20_Opp.pdf: added-evidence, S = 20, n_s = 85. The cumulative
  weight for the false H_123 increases to 0.96.
- Fig2e_evS_equal_123_compl_S20_Opp_N150.pdf: equal-evidence, S = 20, n_s = 150. Each study
  favours the true complement (0.59); the cumulative weight for the complement quickly
  approaches 1.
- Fig2f_evS_added_123_compl_S20_Opp_N150.pdf: added-evidence, S = 20, n_s = 150. The cumulative
  weight for the true complement also approaches 1, but more slowly than in panel e.

Figure 3 - H_123 versus its complement, S = 20 independently generated datasets (n_s = 85);
the studies are ordered by the study-specific support for the selected hypothesis, so the
study-specific weights vary over the studies.
- Fig3a_evS_equal_123_compl_S20_indep.pdf: equal-evidence, H_123 true. 19 of the 20 studies
  favour H_123; the cumulative weight for H_123 ends at 0.80.
- Fig3b_evS_added_123_compl_S20_indep.pdf: added-evidence, H_123 true. The cumulative weight for
  H_123 reaches (practically) 1.
- Fig3c_evS_equal_123_compl_S20_Opp_indep.pdf: equal-evidence, H_123 false. 14 of the 20 studies
  favour the true complement; its cumulative weight reaches (practically) 1.
- Fig3d_evS_added_123_compl_S20_Opp_indep.pdf: added-evidence, H_123 false. The cumulative weight
  for the true complement also reaches (practically) 1.

Figure 4 - H_123 versus its complement, S = 4 independently generated datasets (n_s = 85; the
first four datasets of Figure 3).
- Fig4a_evS_equal_123_compl_S4_indep.pdf: equal-evidence, H_123 true. All four studies favour
  H_123; the cumulative weight for H_123 ends at 0.64.
- Fig4b_evS_added_123_compl_S4_indep.pdf: added-evidence, H_123 true. The cumulative weight for
  H_123 ends at 0.95.
- Fig4c_evS_equal_123_compl_S4_Opp_indep.pdf: equal-evidence, H_123 false. Two of the four
  studies favour the false H_123; the cumulative weight for the true complement ends at 0.94.
- Fig4d_evS_added_123_compl_S4_Opp_indep.pdf: added-evidence, H_123 false. The cumulative weight
  for the true complement ends at 0.61.

Figure 5 - H_123 versus its complement, S = 20 studies with the same dataset (n_s = 85), of
which the last study is an outlier study (estimates in the opposite ordering); the last
study-specific weight therefore deviates from the others.
- Fig5a_evS_equal_123_compl_out_S20.pdf: equal-evidence, H_123 true. The cumulative weight for
  H_123 ends at 0.97 (the outlier study lowers it slightly at the end).
- Fig5b_evS_added_123_compl_out_S20.pdf: added-evidence, H_123 true. The cumulative weight for
  H_123 stays at (practically) 1.
- Fig5c_evS_equal_123_compl_out_S20_Opp.pdf: equal-evidence, H_123 false. The cumulative weight
  for the true complement increases to (practically) 1; the outlier study hardly changes this.
- Fig5d_evS_added_123_compl_out_S20_Opp.pdf: added-evidence, H_123 false. The cumulative weight
  for the false H_123 increases (as in Figure 2d); the outlier study hardly changes this.

Figures 6-8 (simulations)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fig6_sim_weight_true_compl.pdf
  Four panels (top: S = 20; bottom: S = 4; left: H_123 true; right: H_123 false, i.e., the
  complement is true). Per study-specific sample size n_s (x-axis, 25 to 600, with breaks
  between 150 and 300 and between 300 and 600), the median overall GORICA weight for the true
  hypothesis (y-axis, 0 to 1) is shown for four methods next to each other: GORICA on
  fixed-effect (FE) and random-effects (RE) meta-analyzed estimates, equal-evidence (Equal) and
  added-evidence (Added). Whiskers show the 5th to 95th percentile; a dotted line marks 0.5.
  Main pattern: the weights increase with n_s for all methods. When H_123 is true, Added gives
  the highest weights and Equal the lowest; with S = 20, Equal is far below 0.5 for small n_s
  (it then prefers the failsafe) and only approaches 1 for n_s = 300 and 600. When H_123 is
  false, Equal gives the highest weights for the true complement.

Fig7_sim_weight_false_compl.pdf
  Eight panels (top: S = 20; bottom: S = 4; from left to right: FE, RE, Equal, Added). Per n_s
  (x-axis, broken as in Figure 6), the median overall GORICA weight for the selected hypothesis
  (y-axis, 0.5 to 1) is shown for all replications (o), for the true selection cases (t; green),
  and for the false selection cases (f; red), with whiskers (5th to 95th percentile) for the
  true and false selection cases. Red numbers on top give the percentage of false selection
  cases. Main pattern: false selections mainly occur for small n_s, and the weight of the
  selected hypothesis can be well above 0.5 also in false selection cases.

Fig8_sim_KC_THR.pdf
  Three panels with the true hypothesis rate (THR; y-axis, 0 to 1) of the added-evidence (black
  squares) and equal-evidence (orange circles) approach in the simulations of Kuiper and Clapper.
  (a) S = 4: THR per set of hypotheses (x-axis, in the order Sets 1, 4 | 2, 5, 7 | 3, 6, 8, as in
  Kuiper and Clapper); dotted vertical lines separate the sets with hypotheses regarding one
  parameter (1, 4), the sets where the failsafe is not true (2, 5, 7), and the sets where the
  failsafe is (mostly) true (3, 6, 8). (b) and (c) S = 20 without
  an outlier study: THR per n_s (25, 75, 200) for Set 5 (solid lines) and Set 4 (dashed lines),
  when the central hypothesis is true (b) and when it is not true (c). Main pattern: in (a),
  which approach has the higher THR differs per set; in (b), Equal has a much lower THR than
  Added; in (c), Equal has the higher THR.
